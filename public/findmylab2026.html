Intro Problem Ideation & Pivot Research Solution Design Choice Results

Helping 1,500+ UC Davis students find research labs in one place

TLDR

FindMyLab helps UC Davis students find research labs. We first assumed competition was the problem and built a tool for professors, but adoption was low, and our survey showed 90% of students struggled to find research, not win it. I led the pivot to students and designed a single directory of labs, with cards that show what each lab studies and a guide for students who don’t know where to start. Since launch, 1,890 students have used FindMyLab, and 103 of the 388 who searched contacted a lab, in a median of under 2 minutes.

The Problem

Finding a lab shouldn’t depend on who you know

Students without connections or cold-outreach experience struggle to find research labs. Listings are scattered across faculty pages and informal networks. Professors, meanwhile, are behind on email because so many students are writing to ask about lab positions.

North Star Statement

How could we bring every lab into one clear place, so students know where to start and feel ready to reach out?

Ideation & Pivot

Trying the professor side first, then switching to students

We assumed lab positions were hard to get because of competition, so we started with professors, building a triage tool to sort the flood of student emails in their inbox. But our outreach got few responses, and in conversations with about 5 professors, most liked the idea but didn’t want to learn a new tool. When we surveyed students, the real problem became clear.

90% struggled with finding labs,
not competition

Research

Two students, two different starting points

After pivoting to students, we surveyed 200+ students, interviewed about 20, and watched 10–12 of them search for labs. We found two types of students. Student A didn’t know what research they wanted to do yet. Student B knew exactly what they wanted, but had no idea where to find it.

Insight 1

Lab information is scattered across many sources

Even our team needed 25–30 minutes to collect 25 labs, and 43% of students didn’t know where to start.

Design Solution

one searchable directory brings 300 labs into a single place, searchable by major and topic.

Insight 2

Students can’t tell what a lab studies at a glance

In testing, students clicked out to each lab’s website just to read what it researched.

Design Solution

a single line research summary on each card, which cut the time to build a lab list from 5-7 minutes to 2-4 minutes

Insight 3

Labs in the same major can study very different things

26% of students were still choosing a field, so filtering by major alone wasn’t enough.

Design Solution

Topic tags let students browse across departments, with major icons showing each lab’s field at a glance.

Solution

Browsing labs: the card and directory

All labs live in one searchable directory. Each card shows a one-line research summary, a major icon and topic tags, so students can understand and compare labs without leaving the page.

Solution

For students who don’t know research terms yet

Students new to research don’t know which terms to search. Three optional questions ask about their interests in plain language, and AI matches them with relevant labs.

Design Choice

Designing the lab card

Early cards showed only the lab name, professor, major and a link, so students had to visit each lab’s website to learn what it studied. The final card adds a one-line research summary, a major icon, topic tags and an availability badge, letting students evaluate labs without leaving the page. The tradeoff: I kept the summary to one line rather than a full description, giving up detail for a list that’s faster to scan, with full details one click away.

Design Choice

Guiding students who don’t know where to start

43% of students surveyed didn’t know where to start, so search alone wasn’t enough. A banner on the home page opens a short guide that walks students from choosing an interest to emailing a professor, covering the parts of the process that felt most intimidating.

Results

1,890 students, 103 lab contacts, and under 2 minutes

When my team settled on this idea, we had no idea how rewarding it would feel to see students using FindMyLab in our classes and hear it brought up around campus, especially after the long hours we spent reaching out to students and professors to hear what they thought.

This project taught me a lot about working in such an intense environment, and I hope to bring what I’ve learned into future projects. In March 2026, we paused development as team members’ priorities and availability changed. But the site is still live and we have welcomed 500+ students this fall!

Results

My key takeaways from this project

what i’d do differently
Next time, I’d test earlier with both sides. We spent weeks building for professors before asking students what they needed. I’d survey both groups first and A/B test key choices, like how much detail to show on the lab cards.
my next steps
For my next steps, I’d like to refine the design system and add an AI-powered search bar, so students who only have a rough idea of what they’re interested in can still find labs that fit them well.
what i’d learn working with my team
Something I learned while working with a cross-functional team was staying aligned across the whole team. Even though we each had different roles, planning sprints together kept everyone working toward the same goal. Without that shared plan, it’s easy for people to drift toward their own priorities, so regular check-ins helped us catch misalignment early.
